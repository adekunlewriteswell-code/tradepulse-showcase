# TradePulse

### Turning difficult markets into participating markets

**An exploratory demand-activation and market-intelligence concept for FMCG distribution.**

> **Public concept and prototype overview.** This repository presents TradePulse's purpose, product direction, and current limitations. The working prototype, proprietary source code, and operational field data are not published here.

## The problem that inspired TradePulse

Imagine a distribution route with **10 registered outlets**, but only **2 buy occasionally**.

The route exists on a coverage plan, yet most outlets are not generating consistent demand. Sending another representative, repeating the same calls, or dispatching a truck does not necessarily change the underlying economics.

This is an **illustrative field scenario**, not a disclosure of a customer's route data. It represents a problem familiar in difficult or underperforming FMCG markets: **distribution coverage does not automatically create commercial participation**.

Traditional field-sales systems can tell a business which outlets were visited, what was sold, and how targets were achieved. But those records alone may not answer a more fundamental question:

**How do we help more outlets participate in the market, rather than simply revisit the few that already buy?**

TradePulse began with that question.

## The TradePulse hypothesis

Small amounts of genuine demand may exist across outlets that are individually too difficult or uneconomical to serve. If those signals can be captured with minimal friction, assessed together, and linked to credible participation incentives, previously unattractive market pockets might become more viable.

The concept brings together two complementary ideas:

1. **Demand intelligence:** Make early retailer interest visible, explain the evidence, and help teams decide where attention is justified.
2. **Demand activation:** Explore ways to encourage participation through compatible demand groups and conditional, verifiable incentives.

**The ambition is not simply to locate demand. It is to explore how to create better conditions for demand to emerge and become commercially actionable.**

## How the concept could work

| Step | What happens | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Capture** | Retailers or authorized contributors express small, specific demand through low-friction channels, potentially including USSD. | Participation should not depend on a smartphone, expensive data, or a complex workflow. |
| **2. Combine** | Compatible observations are grouped by market, product, unit, and collection window. | Scattered interest becomes easier to evaluate as a collective opportunity. |
| **3. Evaluate** | Teams inspect quantities, distinct contributors, and configured review thresholds. | A threshold is a **signal for commercial review**, not an automatic delivery order. |
| **4. Activate — proposed** | Conditional group incentives could encourage additional outlets to participate, subject to genuine, verified purchases and clear eligibility rules. | Incentives would aim to improve participation, not manufacture misleading demand. |
| **5. Learn** | Compare expressed demand with actual fulfillment and repeat participation, where reliable evidence becomes available. | The model should improve through field validation rather than assumptions. |

Participation incentives and fulfillment-linked rewards are **future product hypotheses**, not features claimed to be running in the current prototype.

## What the prototype explores today

The private prototype includes or demonstrates:

- **Structured demand submissions** with validation and a lightweight contribution flow.
- **Demand grouping and review thresholds** for compatible market/product/unit signals.
- **Market intelligence views** that summarize patterns across groups, markets, and products.
- **Contributor inspection** to examine the observations behind a displayed opportunity.
- **USSD-oriented and simulated workflows** that explore accessible contribution channels.

These are exploratory capabilities. A working demonstration is not proof of a production USSD service, verified purchases, distributor fulfillment, or an operating incentive scheme.

## How TradePulse differs from RouteCRM

| RouteCRM — field-sales execution | TradePulse — market participation and demand activation |
| --- | --- |
| Manages assigned outlets, visits, sales, inventory, and field operations. | Explores how more outlets can express demand and become commercially engaged. |
| Shows what a sales team did and what it achieved. | Investigates where demand could develop and what evidence supports acting on it. |
| Improves execution within an established distribution operation. | Explores opportunities before committing field effort, stock, or delivery resources. |

TradePulse is intended as a **standalone concept**, not a renamed RouteCRM module.

> **In simple terms:** RouteCRM helps digitize and manage the distribution *push*. TradePulse explores how to organize—and responsibly stimulate—the market *pull*.

## Validation and safeguards

TradePulse remains a **prototype and research direction**, not a commercially validated demand-activation network.

Before incentives or fulfillment recommendations could become operational, the concept would need evidence-based validation of:

- **Signal authenticity:** Detecting duplicate, fabricated, stale, or coordinated submissions.
- **Economic viability:** Confirming that grouped demand is genuinely worth serving after delivery and operating costs.
- **Incentive integrity:** Preventing gaming, ensuring fair eligibility, and rewarding only verifiable outcomes.
- **Privacy and trust:** Protecting contributor information and limiting unnecessary disclosure.
- **Field usability:** Testing adoption where devices, bandwidth, digital literacy, and distribution conditions vary.
- **Actual outcomes:** Measuring fulfilled demand and sustained retailer participation, rather than relying on dashboard totals alone.

No confidential customer lists, commercial metrics, operational identities, private credentials, or proprietary source code are included in this public showcase.
